Garden flat · experiment

Living room as a Gaussian splat

No modelling, no textures: the room is rebuilt from eight phone photos as hundreds of thousands of small coloured blobs. It looks like the photos because it was fitted to them.

    Where it holds up. At the photo viewpoints it is close to a photograph, light and all. Within a step of them it stays recognisable but softens. Further away (try free roam), whatever the photos never saw (behind the sofa, under the table, the far side of the room) turns into haze and streaks.

    How it was made. Camera positions from the photos with COLMAP, then trained on a CPU with OpenSplat (no GPU in this environment), 6,000 steps at about 1000 × 750 px. Only the eight photos that overlap enough could be placed; the three looking east share too little with the rest, so that half of the room is missing.

    What would make it walkable. A slow phone video around the whole room (a few hundred overlapping frames) trained on a GPU with nerfstudio/gsplat. That fills the gaps and sharpens everything; the viewer stays the same.
